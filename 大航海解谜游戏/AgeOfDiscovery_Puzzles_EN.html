Common rules (all options)

Teams

  • 5 teams × 5 players. Mix people who don't usually work together; cross-team talk works better that way.
  • One dining table per team serves as its "ship" or "trading house". Put a name card on it; keep all props in one tray or box, covered until the start.
  • Each team picks a "captain" who requests hints and submits answers. Everyone else self-organises.

Hints

  • 3 free hints per team (3 "message in a bottle" cards). After that, each hint costs a 2-minute penalty (in Option B: 2 coins).
  • Every puzzle has 3 hint levels: ① direction → ② method → ③ nearly the answer. Always start at ①.
  • If a team is stuck for more than 5 minutes, the host may offer a free level-① hint.

Each option fine-tunes these times on its own tab. Hard rule: Act 3 must start before 50'. If you're behind, escalate the hints.

Restaurant checklist

  • One day before, confirm: number of tables, places to hide props (kitchen door, bar, under the head table), and how long service takes.
  • Agree a cue with the kitchen (e.g. the host rings a bell); the first dish should come out within 2–3 minutes.
  • Keep props away from cutlery and food. Don't lay cutlery before the game, or cover it with a cloth.
  • Option B uses real spices: smell only, never taste. Put them in opaque jars with pierced lids, and ask about allergies beforehand.

What the opening video must establish

  • Era and place (the Age of Discovery, around 1500)
  • Who the players are (a fleet / trading houses / a crew)
  • Goal and deadline ("before dawn", "before the King's banquet", "before the water runs out")
  • One line hinting at cooperation, to set up the finale twist

A · The Five Fragments

Story: In 1519 a fleet of five ships prepares to leave Lisbon in search of the legendary Spice Islands. The old Admiral dies the night before departure. Fearing a traitor who wants the route for himself, he tears the only chart into five pieces and locks one in each ship's captain's chest.

Tonight the five ships regroup in a harbour: this restaurant. They must rebuild the full route before dawn, or the supplies run out and the fleet disbands.

Suggested closing line for the video: "No fragment is complete. And no key is on its own ship."

Intra

A2 · The crew roster (logic grid)

~8 min · digit 2

All five teams get the same puzzle. Best for 2–3 people working a grid together.

Player text
Four sailors (Pedro, Juan, Diego and Luis) sleep in bunks 1–4. Their jobs are navigator, cook, carpenter and gunner (one each).
① The navigator sleeps in bunk 1.
② The cook's bunk number is exactly one higher than Juan's.
③ Luis sleeps in the last bunk.
④ Diego is neither the carpenter nor the cook.
⑤ The gunner and the carpenter are not in adjacent bunks.
Q: Which bunk does the cook sleep in?
Answer: 3

Luis = 4 (③). If Diego is the navigator, he's in bunk 1, and Juan and Pedro take bunks 2 and 3. If Juan = 3, the cook is in bunk 4 (Luis), and Juan (3) and Pedro (2) would be carpenter and gunner in adjacent bunks, which breaks ⑤. So Juan = 2, and Pedro is in bunk 3 as the cook. If Diego were the gunner, every arrangement puts the gunner next to the carpenter, so that's ruled out.

The cook is Pedro, in bunk 3. (Whether Juan or Luis is the carpenter can't be determined, and it doesn't affect the answer.)

Hints
  1. Draw a 4×4 grid and fill in what's certain.
  2. Start from ③ and ①, then test each possible job for Diego.
  3. Diego is the navigator; Juan is in bunk 2.

Act 2 · Cross-team: the fleet regroups (25–45')

Each team opens its chest and finds two things: the port name on its own fragment is encrypted, and the chest also holds a key card and a seal that aren't theirs. The host announces: "Every key card names its ship on the back. Send out your messengers!"

Messenger rule (optional, to get people moving): each team may have only one messenger away from its table at a time. Key cards may be shown but not handed over, so the other team has to copy them or decode on the spot.

Cross-team

A5 · The route order

~5 min

Once all five ports are decoded, the whole room has to agree the order. The coastlines on the fragment edges fit together, or players can reason from geography: leave Lisbon, round Africa's southern tip, reach India, pass through the Strait of Malacca, arrive at the Spice Islands.

Correct order: Lisbon → Cape → Goa → Malacca → Moluccas

The five half-sentences in order read: "Follow the scent of spice / through the Cape of Storms / across the monsoon sea / past the narrow strait / the feast waits at the end." The last fragment marks an "X" labelled "kitchen door" (or whatever hiding spot you choose).

B · The Seville Spice Exchange

Story: Seville, 1520s, when spice is worth more than gold. The King announces that the first trading house to present every spice in the "Royal Banquet Recipe" wins the royal charter for the next great voyage.

Five trading houses crowd into the Exchange. Each controls the source of only one spice, so none can complete its recipe alone. There will be trades, alliances, betrayals… and pirates.

Twist: in the end, everyone discovers the King's recipe needs the seals of all five houses.
Suggested closing line for the video: "In this Exchange there are no permanent rivals, only deals not yet made."

  • Each house starts with 10 coins (poker chips) and a "house recipe card".
  • The one foreign crate each house receives is exactly the spice its own recipe doesn't need, so it has to be traded.
  • Supply is deliberately short: only 3 crates of each spice circulate, but 4 houses need it. The gap is filled by saffron from the double-lock chests (a wildcard that counts as any spice) and by the Exchange bank. The competition is real.
  • Exchange bank (run by a clerk): sells spice at 4 coins per crate (3 crates total available), buys at 2 coins per crate. Only houses holding a Port Pass may trade with the bank.

Act 3 · Finale: the Royal Recipe (42–52')

Finale

B7 · The King's banquet

~8 min

At 42' the host closes the market and each house scores: +10 for a completed recipe, +1 per remaining coin.

Then the King's envoy (the host) arrives and reads a decree:

"The King will accept only one recipe. It has been split into five parts, written at the foot of the five house recipe cards."

The foot of each recipe card carries a sentence fragment numbered in Roman numerals, plus a seal letter:

HouseFragmentSeal
RoseI · The King's feastS
LionII · belongs toP
StarIII · no single houseI
DolphinIV · but toC
EagleV · all who sailed homeE

Lined up I–V, the cards read "The King's feast belongs to no single house, but to all who sailed home". The seals spell SPICE, which opens the royal chest (5-letter word lock).

What's inside the royal chest
  • A royal charter granted to "all five houses together"
  • A card: "Deliver all your spices to the royal kitchen." The five captains carry their crates to the kitchen door together, the chef takes them and brings out the main course
  • Individual awards: Best Captain (highest score at 42') and Most Generous Merchant (voted by the other houses)

Into dinner

Agree a "royal spice dish" with the kitchen as the main course, e.g. spiced roast chicken, curry, biryani or five-spice beef, ideally using all five spices. Print on the menu: "Royal Banquet Recipe: pepper, cinnamon, clove, nutmeg, star anise".

Props list

  • 25 spice jars (5 teams × 5 spices) + the five ground spices
  • Spice "crate" cards: 3 per spice in circulation + 3 bank reserve + 4 saffron (coloured cards or small matchboxes)
  • About 100 coins (poker chips)
  • Ledger cards ×5 (different altered line per team), currency cards ×5, cargo description cards ×5
  • House recipe cards ×5 (fragment and seal letter printed at the foot), Port Passes ×5
  • Double-lock chests ×2 (3-digit locks), royal chest ×1 (5-letter word lock)
  • Event cards ×6, timelines ×2, pirate chart ×1 + intel cards ×2
  • A small bell (open / close / announcements), a whiteboard for live prices

Host notes

  • The clerks make or break this option: one runs the bank and checks answers, the other hands out rewards and keeps score.
  • A house may hoard and refuse to sell. Don't stop it; the 32' blockade will get goods moving. If things go quiet, bring the 38' pirate raid forward.
  • Don't hint at "cooperation" before the twist. Let them genuinely compete. The twist only lands if the competition came first.

C · The Last Voyage

Story: The ocean-going ship Esperança wakes after a night of storms. The Captain is missing, the ship is off course, and there is only an hour of fresh water left.

All 25 players are crew on the same ship, split into five departments. Each department holds only part of the picture. To find the Captain and the way home, every department needs the others.

The truth: the Captain staged his own disappearance as a test.
Suggested closing line for the video: "The Captain left one message: 'When you find each other, you will find me.'"

Navigators

C1 · Longitude from a clock

~6 min
"Our home port lies on the 0° meridian. The ship's clock always keeps home-port time.
Today, when the sun reached its highest point (local noon), the clock read 16:00.
The Earth turns 15° every hour and the sun moves east to west. Are we east or west of home, and by how many degrees?"
Answer: 60° West (60°W)
Local noon is 4 hours behind home → 4 × 15° = 60°, to the west.
Also received (can't be solved yet)
Sextant record: "Noon on ____: sun altitude 83°." (the date is washed out by seawater)
Formula card: latitude = 90° − sun altitude + the sun's declination that day
Hints

  2. Multiply the hour difference by 15°.
  3. 16 − 12 = 4 hours.

Route answer: → → ↑ ↑

From Esperança (10°N, 80°W) to Echo Island (30°N, 60°W) is 2 squares north and 2 east. Going north first hits the reef at 20°N 80°W; one step east then north hits the reef at 20°N 70°W. The only shortest route is East, East, North, North, i.e. "right, right, up, up" on the directional lock.
